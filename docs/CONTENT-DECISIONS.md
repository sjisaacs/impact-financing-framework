# Content Decisions for v2

## Objective
The framework has one objective: the most durable impact per dollar of scarce concessional capital.
Ratio: size of the change × duration beyond the counterfactual × probability of success × value per unit ×
counterfactual contribution, over expected cost to the funder. Precision acts on the denominator, durability on the
numerator. This is deliberately compatible with cost-effectiveness analysis used by evaluators such as GiveWell (units
of value, stated as multiples of GiveWell's benchmark, which GiveWell called multiples of cash until November 2025).

## The binary, sharpened
- Kept: Transitional Access Gap vs Structural Misalignment.
- Defined by whether the target state is self-enforcing (walk-away test), i.e. whether the externality binds on the
  path (transitional) or at the destination (structural).
- Superseded in the November 2026 review (see below): donors and governments buying on others' behalf are buyers, not
  structural payers.
- "Converging misalignment" moved into transitional gaps (learning curves create two equilibria). v1 filed solar both ways.
- Structural misalignment has two forms: harm that pays (toxic pit) and good nobody pays for (no pit).
- Two sources of the wedge: third-party effects and non-excludable value. Needs without purchasing power are a
  distribution question, not a market failure. Tobacco is mostly an internality.

## Geometry of the externality (new)
The externality is social minus private value; what separates the low points is its slope at the market's low point
(the wedge). Wedge sizes the instrument; displacement sizes the behavior change; welfare at stake (deadweight loss,
exact sense) sizes the case. A uniform externality moves nothing.

## Mechanisms (kept and extended)
Kept v1's two pathways and six levers, canonical cases (corrected), easy vs hard levers, transfers and innovation
incentives, and the catalyst. Added: durability condition per lever (risk transfer is the least durable, so the drift
toward it is also a drift toward the least durable lever), price per unit of outcome (resolves v1's two open edges),
lever accounting as a method and the organizing principle of the case library.

## Repurposed from v1
Foundations (The Gap, Key Concepts, Common Mistakes), the two-landscapes section, the two failure cards and their
solution boxes, Anatomy of an Instrument (three questions, spectrum bars, verification), the Catalyst band, the
Principle of Precision (starting question and six guardrails, now mapped to ratio terms), Bridge to Nowhere, glossary
definitions, instrument strengths and limitations, visual design, illustrations.

## Changed or removed, and why
- Regulation "can't make unprofitable things profitable": removed; regulation is one way to terraform.
- "Same discipline" claim: reframed as "same structure, different stages".
- "Deadweight Loss (Intervention)": renamed Windfall; deadweight loss now used in its exact sense.
- Voluntary carbon credits as the flagship structural example: replaced by compliance carbon pricing and the BC case
  (voluntary credits do not change the emitter's price, and the market contracted sharply).
- Starting question "if private capital eventually solves it, wait": now accounts for timing (acceleration has value).
- Instrument "DNA" percentages: removed (invented precision; "cost of capital 90%" read literally).
- Mode 1/Mode 2, Blocked Flow, Successful Flow, Wrong Destination: retired. Image3 (near-duplicate of Image0) dropped.
- Case studies: kept TLD (corrected) and Frontier (updated); replaced solar, voluntary carbon markets and tobacco with
  implants, the pneumococcal AMC and British Columbia's carbon tax.

## Added in the November 2026 implementation round
- **Worked example and calculator** on the Cost-Effectiveness page. Hypothetical by design and labeled as such. It
  contrasts a headline claim (60 to 1) with an adjusted estimate (5.4 to 1) of the same deal, to show that the inflation
  sits in the numerator and mostly in the counterfactual, and that expected cost is far below headline commitment.
  Results are in payer dollars redeployed per funder dollar; converting to deaths averted or multiples of cash needs
  the payer's marginal cost-effectiveness, which the page says.
- **Diagnostic step 6** turns the ratio into an optional back-of-envelope range with low and high counterfactual
  cases, and adds flags for full attribution, certain success, face-value savings and long credited durations.
  Diagnosis logic now distinguishes "possibly structural" and "transitional, if entry holds" when no payer is planned.
- **Inline definitions** (`<Term>`) for first uses of key terms, answering the critiques' most repeated request without
  a separate beginner track. Definitions come from the glossary, so there is one source.
- **Four instruments restored** from v1, without unsourced numbers: offtake agreements (the energy and climate
  counterpart of a volume guarantee), tiered pricing (a cross-subsidy, not a competitive price), priority review
  vouchers (an innovation incentive that rewards approval, not access) and voluntary carbon credits (a voluntary payer).
- **Corrective taxes** are described as raising the floor under the harmful activity, never as moving a "peak".
- **Valleys kept.** The home page now says plainly that low ground means high returns.

## Steve's review, November 2026
- **Payers and buyers.** Inability to pay is not a market failure; treating it as structural would make almost every
  market structural. Donors, governments and pooled funds that buy on others' behalf are buyers inside the market.
  "Payer" is reserved for whoever corrects a structural gap (externality or public good). The Payers page, the
  nested-markets figure (axis now "buyer at scale"), the wedge sources, the implants, TLD and PCV cases, the
  Cost-Effectiveness page and the diagnostic follow this. The "Diagnose Gaps, Not Markets" section was removed;
  "When Costs Fall with Volume" stays.
- **First illustration.** Water is capital and appears only on the financial landscape. The social landscape shows
  value; an annotation marks where the same capital sits on it (high ground, the misaligned case) and where social
  value is highest. Three cases follow: aligned and capital there (no problem), aligned but stuck (transitional),
  misaligned (structural).
- **Levers.** Lever 2 is "scale and learning": economies of scale (volume per period, lost if volume falls) and the
  experience curve (cumulative output, largely kept). Process improvement is different in kind for durability;
  competition is often fragile. The easy/hard levers section and its figure were removed. The cost/margin figure is
  now a horizontal bar so levers read 1 to 6 in order.
- **Financial transfers.** A buy-down is time-limited by design; the section is about permanent subsidies, and
  distinguishes financial transfers from risk transfer.
- **Language.** No "bind"; no references to earlier versions on content pages; economists introduced with background;
  the wedge credited to Pigou.
- **The catalyst** has its own page, framed as the bridge between simple rational-actor models and how markets behave.
  It separates two kinds of departure, because they need different responses: rational behavior with misaligned
  incentives or incomplete information (principal-agent problems, information asymmetry, incomplete contracts,
  coordination; fixed by changing incentives and information), and behavioral departures from rational choice
  (bounded rationality, loss aversion and status quo bias, defaults; addressed through framing, sequencing and
  defaults). Never present an incentive problem as a behavioral bias.

## Added after the November 2026 critical review
- **Counterfactual contribution replaces "share attributable".** The reviewer's point: the worked example assigned 50%
  because two other partners were needed, which is credit-sharing, not counterfactual impact. The term is now worked
  out by scenario (deal fails without this funder vs another funder steps in), net of what co-funders' money would have
  done elsewhere, matching GiveWell's leverage and funging adjustments. Credit shares are kept for reporting only.
- **Value per unit split in two.** The worked example's 0.8 blended the share of savings redeployed with the value of
  what it buys, and valued the rest at zero. It now values the redeployed share and the returned share separately and
  carries the example through to multiples of the benchmark (headline 120x, adjusted 17x), with sensitivities to the
  counterfactual, the buyer's marginal dollar and the contribution.
- **Implants retrospective.** Chosen over TLD for its cleaner record: a single instrument, a dated competitor, published
  volumes and spending. The counterfactual price paths are drawn from the product's own pre-deal trend, the competitor's
  prequalification date and comparable-market evidence on entry. What buyers would have spent is bracketed, not
  assumed. Because the partners' costs are not public, the result is stated as the break-even cost at GiveWell's 6x bar,
  plus one illustrative cost. All judgments are labeled on the page and the model is re-run by the check script.
- **Calculator and diagnostic** now take value in benchmark dollars and a counterfactual contribution; the diagnostic
  flags round contribution shares that may be credit splits.
