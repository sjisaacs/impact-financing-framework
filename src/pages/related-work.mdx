**The Market Shaping Accelerator at the University of Chicago.** Founded by Michael Kremer and Rachel Glennerster with Christopher Snyder of Dartmouth, it develops pull mechanisms such as advance market commitments and applies them beyond vaccines, including to climate. Its [launch announcement](https://marketshaping.uchicago.edu/news/uchicago-launches-market-shaping-accelerator/) and its [partnership with the Center for Global Development](https://www.cgdev.org/node/3134034) describe the program. Its work is the natural reference for the design of pull mechanisms; this framework adds the question of where they sit relative to a payer.

**The Center for Global Development.** CGD's 2005 working group report, *Making Markets for Vaccines*, set out a detailed design for the advance market commitment, and its continuing work on pull financing informs the [pneumococcal case](/cases/pneumococcal-amc/).

**Global health market shaping practice.** Gavi, Unitaid, the Global Fund, the Clinton Health Access Initiative and others have built the practice this framework draws on, including the case of [contraceptive implants](/cases/implants/) and [TLD](/cases/tld/).

**Blended finance.** Development finance institutions have articulated principles for blended concessional finance, including additionality and minimum concessionality, that closely match the precision guardrails on the [cost-effectiveness page](/cost-effectiveness/#precision-and-durability). Practitioner networks maintain detailed catalogues of blended finance instruments and transactions.

**Carbon removal procurement.** [Frontier](https://frontierclimate.com/assets/annual-update-2025.pdf) applies advance market commitment logic to durable carbon removal, and is discussed in [its case](/cases/frontier/).

**Economic foundations.** The distinction between path and destination externalities draws on Paul Rosenstein-Rodan's work on coordination and the "big push," Kenneth Arrow's on learning by doing, and Arthur Pigou's on corrective taxes.
