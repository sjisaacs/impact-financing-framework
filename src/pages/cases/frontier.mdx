## The Commitment

Frontier launched in 2022 as an advance market commitment by a group of technology and consulting firms to buy durable carbon removal. In mid-2026 its buyers added $915 million, bringing the coalition's total commitment to $1.8 billion. By then its portfolio held about 1.8 million tons of contracted removal across 53 projects, worth $698 million.

## The Turn Toward the Payer

The new phase narrows Frontier's purchasing to fewer companies it judges capable of reaching very large scale. It also adds a demand-side requirement: each deal should have a clear path to reliable long-term demand, whether through compliance markets, industrial regulation or direct government procurement. In its 2025 letter to buyers, Frontier named its main worry as whether demand would keep pace as companies scale.

## Analysis

**Diagnosis.** The market has two gaps. The transitional gap is the learning curve: early removal costs far more than later removal should. The structural gap is that a ton removed has almost no private value to anyone, because the benefit is a global public good that no one can be charged for. Falling costs close only the first gap, so cost reduction alone cannot make the market self-sustaining.

**What the framework predicts.** A transitional instrument applied to a market without a structural payer risks becoming a <Term>bridge to nowhere</Term>. Frontier's shift toward compliance and public demand matches the framework's advice to secure a payer, or a credible path to one, who will buy removal once costs have fallen.

**Cost-effectiveness.** The duration term depends on a payer that does not yet exist, so the probability of success includes a policy outcome as well as a technical one. An estimate should model scenarios for when, and at what price, durable demand arrives, and credit the coalition with how much it speeds up the cost decline in each scenario.
