## The Problem

By 2017, a fixed-dose combination of tenofovir, lamivudine and dolutegravir, known as TLD, offered a better first-line HIV regimen than the efavirenz-based combinations then in wide use, which cost around $100 per person per year. Generic versions from Mylan and Aurobindo had received tentative US approval, and the Medicines Patent Pool provided the licensing channel. But manufacturers faced the fixed costs of gearing up for high volumes, and countries could not commit to switching without assured supply at a predictable price.

## The Intervention

In September 2017, the governments of South Africa and Kenya announced the agreement with UNAIDS, the Clinton Health Access Initiative, the Gates Foundation, Unitaid, the Global Fund and US and UK agencies. The ceiling prices applied to public-sector purchasers in the 92 countries covered by the originator's licensing agreement, home to more than 90 percent of people living with HIV in low- and middle-income countries. The Gates Foundation guaranteed minimum sales volumes, and Bill Gates told Reuters the guarantee was needed because of the fixed costs of preparing for high-volume production.

## Analysis

**Diagnosis.** The buyers were already in place: national programs and their funders buy HIV treatment and are expected to keep doing so. The transitional gap was the <Term t="Coordination Failure">coordination problem</Term> between suppliers investing in capacity and countries committing to switch, and once both happened the market could sustain itself.

**Levers.** The guarantee worked mainly through risk transfer and scale, removing the demand risk on the fixed costs of capacity. The ceiling also left room for competition, since more licensed generic suppliers could enter and bid below it.

**Value per unit.** Buyers benefited in two ways: the new regimen was cheaper than the one it replaced, and its clinical advantages reduce the need for costlier second-line treatment. The value of both depends on how buyers redeploy what they save.

**The counterfactual.** Licensed generic dolutegravir would likely have arrived without the agreement. The agreement's impact is how much sooner countries switched to TLD at the lower price than they otherwise would have.

**Contribution.** Many partners contributed, and each could fairly claim a role. A funding decision should use each partner's <Term>counterfactual contribution</Term>, the difference its participation made. That depends on whether another partner would have stepped in or the deal would have failed, and in the second case on what co-funders' money would otherwise have done. Those contributions need not add up to the whole price drop, so reports divide credit by other means ([see the terms](/cost-effectiveness/#counterfactual-contribution)).
