title: Open Questions
description: The research agenda the framework generates, published to invite practitioners and economists to test and improve it.
lede: A framework earns its keep by predicting outcomes, not just describing them. These are the questions where it most needs testing, and where contributions would be most useful.
---

1. **Predicting self-enforcement.** What evidence, gathered before an intervention, reliably shows that a better equilibrium exists? The walk-away test is only as good as this answer.
2. **When structural gaps stop mattering.** Solar's unpriced climate benefit mattered less once costs fell. Which other structural gaps can transitional work dissolve, and how would you know in advance?
3. **Durable payers.** What design features help permanent payers survive political change, such as earmarked revenue, household rebates, multilateral pooling or legal commitments?
4. **Standards for lever accounting.** How should a price change be attributed across levers when cost data are partial and the counterfactual is uncertain?
5. **What called guarantees reveal.** How often are guarantees called, and does a called guarantee reliably signal a misdiagnosed structural gap?
6. **Exposure to buyer retreat.** Which health markets would lose their market-shaping gains first if donor-financed volume falls?
7. **Outcome units where outcomes are contested.** Price per unit of outcome works for viral suppression or tons removed. How should it work where the outcome itself is disputed, as in mental health?
8. **Who pays the catalyst.** Coordination and market intelligence are public goods. Is the catalyst itself a structural gap, and who should fund it?

If you are working on any of these, or have evidence that bears on them, please get in touch through the [feedback link](/about/#feedback).
